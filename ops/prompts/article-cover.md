Create the cover image for one article on the blog of a small Japanese B2B company that builds and runs websites, marketing automation and customer-support systems for small and mid-sized businesses.

Work as two specialists at once:
- A senior corporate graphic designer, who keeps every cover on-brand so that all covers read as one series with the website's hero (a handcrafted wood-and-paper model).
- An SNS marketer, who needs the image to stop the scroll as a small link-preview thumbnail on X, Facebook, LinkedIn and LINE, and to make the topic clear before anyone reads the title.

Article title (Japanese): {{title}}
Article summary (Japanese): {{description}}

1. Pick ONE visual metaphor (decide it silently; do not render any words)
Find the reader's core problem or the article's single takeaway in the title and summary, then show it as one concrete physical scene made of a few objects. Good starting points:
- Comparison / how to choose: three or four clearly different paper models side by side on the board, with one of them gently picked out by warm light and a small blue paper marker.
- Problems / failures / scattered work: tangled thread and scattered blank paper slips on one side, the same items neatly sorted into a wooden tray on the other side (before → after).
- Steps / process / getting started: a short path of paper stepping stones or wooden blocks leading to one small goal object.
- Systems / integration / data flow: a few paper parts joined by thin thread, with tiny points of blue light travelling along the thread.
- Growth / results / improvement: wooden blocks or folded paper steps rising gently toward the light.
A viewer must understand the idea in under one second, without the title. Do not illustrate the title word by word.

2. Render it in the house style
- A handcrafted miniature diorama, photographed like a premium tabletop product shot: a light natural-wood base board, matte off-white paper and cardstock parts with visible folds and clean cut edges, small wooden blocks, thin cotton thread.
- Light: warm, soft lamp light from the upper left, gentle soft shadows, slight shallow depth of field. Calm, trustworthy and modern. Photographic realism, not a vector illustration, not a glossy 3D render.
- Colour: mostly warm wood, off-white paper and a plain warm off-white background. Brand colours only as small accents: deep navy (#003577) and bright blue (#0581DB) on one or two key paper pieces, markers or tiny points of light. At most one tiny warm-gold accent to mark the answer or the good outcome. No other saturated colours.

3. Compose for every place the image is shown
- The image is cropped to 16:9 on the blog, to 1.91:1 for link previews and to 2:1 in the X timeline. Keep everything important inside the central horizontal band: leave the top 18% and the bottom 18% of the frame free of important objects (only plain background or wood surface there), and keep 8% clear at the left and right edges.
- One clear focal point, large enough to read when the image is only 300 px wide. Five to seven objects at most. Strong light–dark contrast between the subject and the background so the shape still reads at thumbnail size.
- Slightly raised three-quarter camera angle (about 30 degrees), focal point slightly off-centre, balanced and generous negative space, no busy background.

Strict rules
- No text of any kind anywhere: no letters, Japanese characters, numbers, words, captions, labels, symbols or fake interface text, including on paper, boxes, screens and signs. Paper may show only blank lines or empty grid cells.
- No people, faces, hands or other body parts, no silhouettes or icons of people, no dolls or figurines.
- No logos, brand marks, trademarks, product screenshots, devices showing an interface, or any recognisable company or product design.
- No generic business icons (gears, clouds, databases, bar-chart icons, magnifying glasses, light bulbs, arrows), no floating UI cards, no glossy cubes, no isometric clip art, no neon or sci-fi style.
- No watermarks, signatures, frames or borders.
